# Plogger Method — Parallel-Track Practice Plan

> Executive Summary · Full chapter-by-chapter detail to follow

---

## The Core Insight

The Plogger book *appears* linear but actually has **three types of content** that require different cognitive modes:

- **Declarative** (know that): Pythagorean ordering, di-chord names, interval tables — memorization drills
- **Perceptual** (hear that): Interference pulsation, F/O direction, harmonicity — ear rewiring
- **Motor** (do that): Lap Map, keyboard visualization, clef reading, solfège — physical automaticity

These three modes don't interfere with each other neurologically. You can run one of each type simultaneously without cognitive overload. That's the basis for the parallel structure.

---

## Five Permanent Tracks

### Track M — Mental Framework *(Ch. 1–2)*

Read Ch. 1 and Ch. 2 once, then *apply them as a lens to every other exercise forever*. This isn't a track you "finish" — it's the meta-layer. Before every session: which Stage are you in? After errors: which of the 3 causes? This takes zero extra time and amplifies every other track.

### Track R — Rhythm *(Ch. 4–5)*

Longy syllables + Lap Map. Fully independent of pitch — you can practice it anywhere, any time, no instrument needed. It runs in parallel with every other track permanently and never stops. This is the one track that has zero prerequisites and zero conflicts with anything else.

### Track G — Geography *(Ch. 3 → Ch. 6 → Ch. 7–8 → Ch. 25)*

Keyboard visualization → Pythagorean ordering → Interval spelling (keyboard then staff) → Transposition. Sequential within the track but completely parallel to Tracks R and D. Ch. 6 Pythagorean ordering is a daily recitation habit that takes ~90 seconds and can be done without an instrument.

### Track D — Di-Chord / Sonic Properties *(Ch. 9 → Ch. 10–14 → Ch. 15–16)*

Di-chord numbers → Three sonic properties → Melodic contexts → Tracking page. This is the intellectual heart of the method. Ch. 9 is the critical prerequisite (without it, nothing in Ch. 10–16 makes sense), but Ch. 9 itself has no prerequisite except the keyboard geography from Ch. 3. So Track D waits only for Track G to reach Ch. 3.

### Track S — Structure *(Ch. 17 → Ch. 18 → Ch. 19–20 → Ch. 21–24)*

Tri-chords → Tetrachords → Modes/Heptachords → Triads/7th Chords/Harmonization. This track waits for Track D to reach Ch. 9 (you need di-chord numbers to describe structures), but once that gate opens, it runs in parallel with Track D's deeper chapters.

## Dependency Map

```
Day 1:   Track M ████████████████████████████████ (forever)
         Track R ████████████████████████████████ (forever)
         Track G: Ch. 3 starts

Gate 1 — G clears Ch. 3:
         Track G: Ch. 6 starts
         Track D: Ch. 9 starts

Gate 2 — D clears Ch. 9:
         Track D: Ch. 10–14 starts
         Track S: Ch. 17 starts    ← both unlock simultaneously

Gate 3 — D clears Ch. 10–14:
         Track D: Ch. 15–16 starts
         (Track G continues: Ch. 7–8)

Gate 4 — S clears Ch. 17–18, G clears Ch. 7–8:
         Track S: Ch. 19–20 (Modes / Heptachords)

Gate 5 — D clears Ch. 15–16, S clears Ch. 19–20:
         Track S: Ch. 21–24 (full harmonic analysis)
```

At peak you are running **5 tracks simultaneously**. On Day 1 you run 3.

## Mastery Gate Criteria

The book gives explicit standards — formalized here into four tests. **All four must pass** before advancing within a track. Tracks advance independently of each other.

| Test | Name | What it means |
|------|------|---------------|
| 1 | **Speed Gate** | Any exercise item answered in under 10 seconds, cold (no warm-up). If you need 15–20 s, you're still in Stage 2. |
| 2 | **12-Key Gate** | For any pitch exercise, complete it starting from all 12 chromatic pitch classes without a single hesitation. One key is a trick; all 12 is fluency. |
| 3 | **Dual-Task Gate** | Can you perform the exercise while simultaneously tapping the Lap Map? If the Lap Map breaks, the pitch exercise isn't Stage 3 yet. |
| 4 | **Cold-Start Gate** | Walk away for 24 hours. Begin immediately the next day without re-studying. No = still consolidating. Yes = automatic. |

## Sample Daily Session Skeleton

*~12 min Plogger section, structured across all active tracks*

| Slot | Track | Content | Time |
|------|-------|---------|------|
| 1 | R | Lap Map drill — today's meter pattern | 2 min |
| 2 | G | Pythagorean ordering recitation (ascending + descending, letters + solfège) | 90 sec |
| 3 | D | Di-chord flashcards or pulsation listening drill | 3 min |
| 4 | S | Current Structure exercise — all 12 keys | 3 min |
| 5 | R + D | Longy exercise while tracking di-chords simultaneously | 2 min |

Track M is silent but always active — you are observing which Stage you are in throughout.

## What This Gives You

- You are never stuck waiting on one thing to unlock everything
- Rhythm and Geography compound daily without requiring instrument time
- The mastery gates are objective and self-administrable — no teacher needed to know when to advance
- The dual-task gate (Lap Map + pitch) naturally aligns with the book's daily session structure
- The 5-track architecture mirrors how the method actually works: Ch. 1–2 say the bottleneck is *conscious direction*, not the ear — running parallel tracks is itself a training in conscious direction
