## What the System Is

A navigational zoom architecture that answers, at the speed of music:

> *"Where am I, what meaningful possibilities surround me, how can I physically play them, where can I move next, and what will each choice feel like?"*

The zoom hierarchy:

```
Galaxy (tuning) → Solar System (key/mode) → Map → Current Note
  → Orbit (root/3rd/5th interpretation)
    → Constellation (playable voicing)
      → Path → Segments → Performance
```

Three explicit model layers:
- **Absolute model** — invariant theory relationships (degree 6 is always root of vi, third of IV, fifth of ii — regardless of tuning or key)
- **Navigational model** — changes with Galaxy (tuning), Solar System (key/mode), hand position, anchor
- **Emotional model** — changes with tonal center, active Constellation, timing, duration, style

The Emotional Coordinate layer (4 zones, 4 arrival behaviors, adapted from Bodzsar 2026) sits on top of all of it.

**The single most architecturally novel decision: the Parallel-Galaxy Doctrine.** Standard and DADGAD are co-primary from Day 1 — never a "learn Standard, transfer later" relationship. Every drill, infographic, and software screen must exist as a matched pair. This is stated as non-negotiable and has deep consequences for how the app must be built.

## Document 1: Integrated Architecture (v2.1)

A production handoff specification — written to be handed to an AI, curriculum designer, infographic producer, or software team. It consolidates all prior AMF Musical Universe work (DADGAD, scale compression, triads, voice-leading, navigation, emotional mapping) into a unified system.

### Structure (15 Sections)

1. **Executive Purpose and Success Criteria** — north star, desired end state, anti-patterns to prevent
2. **Core Philosophy and Non-Negotiable Design Principles** — 9 explicit principles with operational meanings
3. **Unified Vocabulary and Hierarchy** — master glossary (Universe, Galaxy, Solar System, Map, Segment, Path, Orbit, Constellation, Anchor, Bridge, Destination, Emotional coordinate)
4. **Absolute Theory vs. Navigational Geography** — invariant vs. tuning-specific vs. emotional-context layers
5. **Scale and Pentatonic Maps** — diatonic segments L/M/P and pentatonic segments S/L; 7-step and 5-step cycles; modal entry points
6. **Segments, Paths and Nested Musical Resolution** — how paths are assembled from segments; 6-level path decision hierarchy
7. **Harmony Map: Degrees, Orbits and Constellations** — the Orbit rotation table (every degree as root/third/fifth of three chords); Constellation definition at abstract/physical/variant levels
8. **Triads, Open Voicings, Seventh Chords and Extensions** — closed triad inventory; open/spread voicings; seventh chord architecture; Core/Structural/Color/Tension classification
9. **Anchor, Bridge and Voice-Leading Maps** — four anchor types; bridge-generation workflow; voice-leading cost model
10. **Emotional Map Integration** — adapted from Bodzsar 2026; 4 emotional coordinate zones; 4 chord-change arrival behaviors; Emotional Waypoints; changing-backdrop concept
11. **Visual and Interaction Specification** — visual grammar (hue = degree, fill = chord-tone status, etc.); 10-step interaction model for software
12. **Infographic and Atlas Deliverables** — five deliverable sets (foundation, scale/pentatonic, triad/harmony, seventh/extension, voice-leading/anchor/emotion)
13. **Accuracy, QA and Validation** — computational pitch validation; musical review; visual review
14. **Research Backlog and Implementation Phases** — 8 phases (0–7); 7 open research questions
15. **Reference Appendices** — DADGAD string layout; diatonic triad qualities; Emotional Map attribution; AI production directive

### Key Structural Concepts

**The Orbit rotation table as a generator.**
Orbits are not taught as three facts per note — they are generated by modulo-7 rotation: Root Orbit = same degree, Third Orbit = 2 back, Fifth Orbit = 4 back. The entire harmonic identity table is derived from one rule, not memorized.

**The Anchor taxonomy (4 types).**
Shared, Resolving, Color, and Anticipatory anchors — with explicit definitions, likely effects, and use cases. The Voice-Leading Map and Bridge system are both built around this taxonomy.

**Bridge as constrained voice-leading, not substitution.**
The Architecture explicitly rejects "bridge chords as a bag of substitutions" in favor of generating bridges from anchor choice. The bridge-generation workflow is 8 steps, producing bridges from anchor type rather than chord catalog.

**The Emotional Coordinate System.**
Four zones (grounded/sweet/confirmed; floating/open/colorful; tense but harmonically supported; strong friction/suspense) and four arrival behaviors (Confirm, Float, Resolve, Depart). The "changing backdrop" concept — static note, moving Constellations beneath it — is particularly useful for DADGAD and pedal-point scenarios.

**Top-note functional language.**
"Root on top / third on top / fifth on top" as the performance-first label for inversions, replacing "root position / first inversion / second inversion." Connects anchor selection to physical execution without a translation step.

**Emotional Map Waypoints.**
Not every note is analyzed — only notes at chord changes, repeated/static notes, long notes, phrase openings and endings, and accented/anticipated notes. A compression rule for emotional analysis that prevents overload during performance.

**Core/Structural/Color/Tension vertical classification.**
A 4-way classification of a note's vertical status in the active Constellation — richer than typical chord-tone vs. non-chord-tone treatments and directly tied to the Emotional Map and voicing decisions.

**Voice-leading cost model.**
Explicit enumeration of cost metrics: anchor preservation, semitone movement, common tones, stepwise tendency, voice crossing, register displacement, directional pull, emotional displacement. Scaffolded for algorithmic/computational implementation.

## Document 2: Fluency Curriculum (v1.1)

The pedagogical implementation of the Architecture. Where the Architecture says "here is the system," the Curriculum says "here is how to internalize it — in what order, by what gates, at what tempo." A learning roadmap with 11 sequential phases, skill gates, drills, schedules, a troubleshooting table, and a maintenance rotation.

### Structure (15 Sections)

1. **Purpose and Learning Doctrine** — Parallel-Galaxy Doctrine; 3-stage internalization cycle; definition of "fluent" (6 behavioral criteria)
2. **The Fluency Ladder** — 11-rung ordered capability ladder with explicit prerequisite rationale for each rung
3. **Practice Architecture and Gates** — daily block structure; 6-stage skill-gate template; spaced practice rule; synchronized two-track practice rule
4. **Phase 0: Setup and Baseline** — 3–7 days; establish home key, one string set per tuning, record baseline
5. **Phase 1: Degree and Segment Fluency** — 2–4 weeks; degree recognition sequence (1/3/5 → add 2/6 → add 4/7 → randomize → transpose)
6. **Phase 2: Closed Triad Constellations** — 4–8 weeks; "the most important performance foundation"; 6-drill set
7. **Phase 3: Orbit Fluency** — 3–6 weeks; cognitive (rotation rule) and physical (land → choose Orbit → name missing tones → complete Constellation) training
8. **Phase 4: Local Navigation and Inversion Paths** — 4–8 weeks; same-chord navigation, direct chord-to-chord, voice-role listening drills
9. **Phase 5: Anchors and Emotional Coordinates** — 4–6 weeks; emotional ear foundation; 4 anchor-selection drills; static-note/backdrop exercise
10. **Phase 6: Bridge Transitions** — 6–10 weeks; 8-step learning sequence; 5 practice formats
11. **Dual-Galaxy Practice Architecture** — governs all phases; paired-track content matrix; weekly synchronization pattern (5-day cycle); 5-level independent fluency gates; DADGAD-specific overlays
12. **Phase 7: Seventh Chords and Extensions** — 8–16 weeks; expansion sequence; gate requirements before upper extensions
13. **Phase 8: Composition and Spontaneous Use** — creative workflow (9 steps); performance attention rule ("one conscious layer at a time"); 6 composition studies
14. **Assessment, Troubleshooting and Maintenance** — diagnostic symptoms table with causes and corrections; maintenance rotation by week type
15. **Sample Schedules and Drill Library** — 12-week triad-to-Orbit foundation plan; 15-minute micro-session template; 9-item definition-of-done checklist

### The Three-Stage Internalization Cycle

Every concept is trained through:
1. **Recognize** — slow conscious identification; flashcards, diagrams, verbal naming
2. **Use** — quick retrieval without calculation; timed prompts, ear drills, random targets
3. **Fluent** — automatic, serves music without occupying attention; grooves, songs, improvisation, constrained play

**"Fluent" is explicitly defined with 6 behavioral criteria:** 90% accuracy, 2-second response, eyes-closed ability, can hear and describe alternatives, can recover from errors, can use while attention is partially elsewhere.

### The 6-Stage Skill Gate

Every new concept passes through: **Accuracy → Randomization → Time → Ear → Context → Attention**

### The 9-Item Definition of Done

*For any concept introduced in the curriculum:*
1. Can explain it
2. Can see it (visualize the keyboard/fretboard)
3. Can play it slowly
4. Can identify degrees and roles
5. Can hear its broad emotional effect
6. Can retrieve it randomly (no sequential warm-up)
7. Can maintain pulse while using it
8. Can use it in a real musical context
9. Can stop naming it while still using it

### The Fluency Ladder (11 Rungs, with Prerequisite Rationale)

The ordering is not arbitrary — each rung has a stated prerequisite:

| Rung | Capability | Why This Order |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Degree identity (1–7 per tuning) | Foundation for everything |
| 2 | Diatonic + pentatonic segments | Segments must feel like motion units before they become path components |
| 3 | Closed triads in all Constellations | Orbit selection must terminate in an automatic shape |
| 4 | Orbit fluency (rotation rule) | Orbits depend on degree identity |
| 5 | Anchor completion (inversion paths) | Orbit-to-Constellation bridge |
| 6 | Local navigation (same-chord inversions) | In-chord ornamentation requires voicing freedom |
| 7 | Direct chord-to-chord transitions | Bridge shouldn't obscure direct voice-leading |
| 8 | Anchor selection and emotional coordinates | Expressive control requires hearing continuity vs. tension |
| 9 | Bridge transitions | Bridge as constrained voice-leading; rests on anchor fluency |
| 10 | Seventh chords and extensions | Four-note complexity rests on fluent triad identity |
| 11 | Composition and spontaneous use | All of the above |

## Relationship Between the Two Documents

These are complementary halves of a single system:

- The **Architecture** is the specification layer — what the system is, its vocabulary, its data model, its visual grammar, its computational validation requirements, its deliverable list. Written as a handoff to a producer or software team.
- The **Curriculum** is the pedagogical layer — how a student internalizes the system, in what order, at what granularity, with what gates and drills. Written as a teaching roadmap.

The Architecture defines *what exists*. The Curriculum defines *how to build the player's internal representation of it* and in what sequence. Every major structural element in the Architecture has a corresponding Phase in the Curriculum. Both enforce the Parallel-Galaxy Doctrine from Phase 0 onward. Terminology is identical throughout — no drift or inconsistency between documents.

## Mapping to the Existing Four Chambers

The Musical Universe documents do not use Four Chambers language (Melody, Harmony, Voicings, Rhythm) — but the subject matter maps clearly:

| Four Chambers | Musical Universe Equivalent |
|---|---|
| **Melody** | Scale Map + Pentatonic Map; Segments (L/M/P, S/L); Paths; Emotional Waypoints |
| **Harmony** | Harmony Map; Orbit rotation table; diatonic degree system; chord quality taxonomy |
| **Voicings** | Constellation system (closed/open/spread/variants); triad inventory on all string sets; seventh chord architecture |
| **Rhythm** | Listed as a Map type in scope — but minimally developed in both documents. No dedicated section. The clearest gap. |

Whether to **align the vocabularies** (Four Chambers ↔ Galaxy/Orbit/Constellation) or treat them as **two separate conceptual layers** is one of the first design decisions needed before integration.

## What Is Genuinely New vs. What Overlaps

### Genuinely New

- **Parallel-Galaxy Doctrine** — Standard and DADGAD co-primary from Day 1; every deliverable issued as a matched pair. Real architectural constraint, not a pedagogy preference.
- **Orbit rotation as a generator** — modulo-7 rule derives the entire harmonic identity table from one algorithm instead of memorized lookup. Compressible and implementable.
- **Anchor taxonomy (4 types)** — Shared, Resolving, Color, Anticipatory. Clean, original classification that drives the Bridge and Voice-Leading Maps.
- **Bridge as constrained voice-leading** — generated from anchor type, not chosen from a substitution catalog. The 8-step bridge workflow is novel process design.
- **Emotional coordinate system** — 4 zones, 4 arrival behaviors, Emotional Waypoints. Practical compression model that prevents performance overload.
- **Top-note functional language** — "root on top / third on top / fifth on top" as inversion labels; eliminates the naming-to-playing translation step.
- **Changing backdrop concept** — static note, moving Constellations beneath it. Directly useful for DADGAD and pedal-point composition.
- **Core/Structural/Color/Tension vertical classification** — more rigorous than chord-tone / non-chord-tone; ties directly to Emotional Map and voicing decisions.
- **Voice-leading cost model** — 8 explicit cost metrics, scaffolded for computation. Enables algorithmic bridge and anchor generation.
- **The 11-rung Fluency Ladder with prerequisite rationale** — the most pedagogically rigorous ordering rationale in any AMF document to date.
- **The 9-item definition-of-done checklist** — concrete, actionable, testable. Could directly become software gate conditions.

### Overlaps with Existing AMF Content

- **L/M/P Segments and the diatonic 7-step cycle** — core to existing Four Chambers work, carried forward intact
- **The degree-color palette** — referenced as "the established AMF degree-color palette"; pre-exists these documents
- **Triads and inversions on all string sets** — core Voicings Chamber material
- **DADGAD as a tuning environment** — already in AMF; what's new is its elevation to co-primary status
- **Diatonic chord qualities and the harmonized scale** — standard music theory; not novel

## The Plogger Integration Gap

This is the most critical finding for the next design phase.

**Plogger's analytical layer — di-chord numbers, interference pulsation, F/O factor, harmonicity — is completely absent from both Musical Universe documents.** This is not an oversight. The Musical Universe operates at the chord-function and emotional-coordinate level. Plogger operates at the **psychoacoustic substrate level** below that. They are complementary, not competing. But the connection is not drawn anywhere in either document.

The Musical Universe tells a player: *"This note is a Color tone in a Third Orbit IV Constellation — floating, open emotional coordinate."* Plogger could tell them *why* — the specific pulsation rate of that interval stack, its harmonicity, the F/O direction of the interval. One is navigational, one is perceptual physics.

### Natural Integration Points

| Plogger Concept | Where It Connects in Musical Universe |
|---|---|
| **Harmonicity + F/O factor** | Core/Structural/Color/Tension classification (Section 8.4). Currently subjective — Plogger's computed values make it rigorous and automatic. High-harmonicity stacks = Core tones; low-harmonicity = Tension tones. |
| **Interference pulsation** | Constellation voicing metadata. The Architecture calls for computational validation of all voicings — pulsation rate per voicing is a natural addition. Tells you *why* a Constellation sounds dense or open in a specific register. |
| **Di-chord numbers** | Intervals generated by the Orbit rotation rule. Every Orbit transition produces a specific di-chord relationship. Plogger becomes the acoustic explanation of why Root Orbit vs. Third Orbit feels different. |
| **Sonic properties (general)** | Segment emotional metadata. The Architecture already has a field for "expected emotional effect in context" per Segment — Plogger's properties could populate that with acoustic grounding rather than subjective description. |

### What's Missing

The Musical Universe system tells a player what to play and what it will feel like. It does not tell them why specific intervals produce specific sensations. Plogger is precisely that explanation layer. The integration point would make the Emotional Coordinate system *derivable* from acoustic physics rather than learned by rote — the floating/open quality of a Color tone in a Third Orbit becomes explainable by its harmonicity value and pulsation rate.

## Key Terminology Reference

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Galaxy** | A specific tuning and its physical geometry (Standard, DADGAD) |
| **Solar System** | The current tonal/modal world — key + mode + its center of gravity |
| **Map** | A selective information layer over the musical landscape (Scale, Pentatonic, Harmony, Voice-Leading, Bridge, Emotional, Rhythm, Extension) |
| **Segment** | Small reusable unit of interval motion: L (major third, 0-2-4), M (minor third, 0-2-3), P (minor third alt, 0-1-3), S (pentatonic short, 0-2), L (pentatonic long, 0-3) |
| **Path** | Route from current location to destination, assembled from Segments; 6-level decision hierarchy |
| **Orbit** | Harmonic interpretation of the current note: Root, Third, or Fifth (expands to Seventh with extensions) |
| **Constellation** | Playable chord voicing completing a selected Orbit; exists at abstract, physical, and variant levels |
| **Anchor** | Voice held stable through a transition; 4 types: Shared, Resolving, Color, Anticipatory |
| **Bridge** | Temporary Constellation inserted to shape the route between current and destination harmony |
| **Emotional coordinate** | A note's position in stability space — determined by tonal stability × harmonic support |
| **Confirm / Float / Resolve / Depart** | Four chord-change arrival behaviors |
| **Emotional Waypoint** | Notes that disproportionately shape listener interpretation: chord changes, repeated/static notes, long notes, phrase boundaries, accented/anticipated notes |
| **Parallel-Galaxy Doctrine** | Standard and DADGAD trained simultaneously from Day 1; neither is a transfer from the other |
| **Fluency Ladder** | 11-rung ordered capability sequence with prerequisite rationale for each rung |
| **Skill Gate** | 6-stage gate: Accuracy → Randomization → Time → Ear → Context → Attention |
| **Three-stage internalization** | Recognize → Use → Fluent |

## Design Implications for the AMF App

1. **The Orbit generator (modulo-7 rotation rule) is directly implementable** as a data layer — it replaces static lookup tables with a single algorithm. Every note in any Solar System produces 3 Orbit options without hardcoding.

2. **The interaction model in Section 11.2 is a literal UX wireframe spec** — Galaxy selector → Solar System selector → Map toggle → note input → Orbit chooser → Constellation filter → destination/anchor → emotional overlay → progressive label hiding. This maps directly onto what the app needs to build next.

3. **The Fluency Ladder and skill gates are app-native structures.** Each gate condition is testable and could drive a mastery-based progression unlock system in the app.

4. **The Rhythm Map is the most underdeveloped Map** — listed in scope but has no dedicated section in either document. This gap needs specification work before implementation.

5. **The Emotional Map is attributed to Bodzsar 2026 (The Emotional Map of Melody)** — the source text should be reviewed when implementing the emotional coordinate system to understand what was preserved vs. extended.

6. **The 9-item definition-of-done checklist** (Section 15.3 of the Curriculum) could become the software's mastery verification protocol — the app could run through each condition as a practice gate.

7. **Plogger's analytical substrate (di-chord numbers, F/O factor, harmonicity, interference pulsation) has no current connection point in these documents.** Integration requires deliberately mapping those concepts onto the Core/Structural/Color/Tension classification, Segment metadata, and the voice-leading cost model. This should be an explicit architecture decision before implementation begins.

## Open Question Before Integration Begins

**Should Plogger be a visible curriculum layer** — something students actively study alongside Musical Universe material, with its own vocabulary (di-chord numbers, sonic properties, pulsation) surfaced in the UI?

**Or should it be an invisible computational engine** — powering the Orbit/Constellation classifications and emotional coordinates under the hood, without surfacing its own vocabulary to learners?

That distinction determines the entire integration architecture.
