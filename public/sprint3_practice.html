Week 1 · Read Before Practice
Read these summaries before you pick up your instrument. Understanding the concept in words first makes the physical practice 3× faster.
Musical OS · Plogger Ch.5–6

Ch.5 Lap Map — Your Hand Is the Keyboard

Place your left hand palm-down on your lap. Your five fingers represent the five white keys in any pentachord position; the gaps between them represent black keys. This is the Lap Map — a portable, always-available keyboard simulator.

There are four tetrachord types. A tetrachord is a group of four notes spanning a perfect 4th. The four types differ only in where the half-step sits: Ionian (T-T-S — e.g., F G A B♭), Dorian (T-S-T — e.g., G A B♭ C), Phrygian (S-T-T — e.g., E F G A), Lydian (T-T-T — e.g., F G A B). Sprint 3 focuses on Ionian and Dorian because Ipanema lives in F Ionian and uses Gm (Dorian flavor) in the changes.

Ch.6 Pythagorean Ordering of Fifths — F C G D A E B

The sequence F – C – G – D – A – E – B is produced by stacking perfect 5ths. Each step adds one sharp (or removes one flat). Adjacent keys in this chain share 6 of their 7 notes — meaning moving from F major to C major, you only change one note (B♭ → B). This is why key changes along the chain of fifths feel smooth.

Mode brightness tracks position: keys to the right of F (C, G…) are brighter (more sharps); keys to the left (B♭, E♭…) are darker. Ipanema's bridge moves to G♭ — the opposite direction from F on the chain, creating the dreamy half-step disorientation that defines the tune.

Melody Chamber · 5 Key Notes + Tiny Tension Arc

5 Key Notes Framework

Every melody can be analyzed by finding five structural notes: First (how does it launch?), Last (where does it land?), Highest (the peak — often the emotional climax), Longest (duration amplifies zone — the held note declares its zone loudly), Beat-1 Notes (downbeat notes carry harmonic weight). In Ipanema's verse, the melody opens on A (the major 3rd of Fmaj7 — Zone 1), rises to its highest note on the word "watch," and consistently lands on Zone 1 tones at phrase endings.

Tiny Tension Arc — The Hook Engine

A Tiny Tension Arc is a 3–4 note motif that follows: Zone 1 → Zone 1 → Zone 2 → Zone 1. You establish comfort (Z1), rise to mild tension (Z2), then resolve back. This is the micro-grammar of every catchy melody. Ipanema's opening phrase is a textbook example. Hear before you play — sing the arc before touching the instrument.

Rhythm Chamber · Son Clave + Bossa Nova Feel

Son Clave 3-2

Son Clave is a two-bar rhythmic pattern that underlies Latin music. In 3-2 orientation: Bar 1 has 3 hits (beat 1, beat 2-and, beat 3); Bar 2 has 2 hits (beat 1-and, beat 3). The bossa nova guitar and piano rhythms are derived from this pattern — the "and" anticipations you hear in Ipanema come directly from clave positions. Clap it. Feel it. Then play it.

Rhythm Code — Positions 2, 4, 6, 8 as Lift

In the 8-position grid (each beat = positions 1-2, each bar of 4 = 8 positions), the even positions (2, 4, 6, 8) are the "and" beats — landing here creates lift and forward motion. Bossa nova feel depends on anticipating chord changes and melody notes on these "and" positions rather than waiting for the downbeat.

Voicings Chamber · Deepening Inversions

No new voicing types this sprint — the goal is voice economy. Play Fmaj7, Gm7, and C7 using the fewest notes that preserve the quality. Shell voicings (root + 3rd + 7th) are your target. Notice how small movements between chords are possible when you choose inversions wisely. Sing before you play — hear the top voice of each chord before your hand moves.

Lap Map — Ionian Tetrachord from F 3 minS1

Hear before you play. Say aloud: "F G A B♭ — tone tone semitone." Place left hand palm-down on lap. Thumb = F, index = G, middle = A, ring = B♭ (the gap between ring and pinky = the half-step). Tap the pattern: thumb, index, middle, ring — speaking "T-T-S" as you tap. Eyes closed. Repeat 4×. Then do Ionian from C (C D E F). Compare the position of the half-step gap in your hand. Notice: same pattern, different starting finger.

Dorian Tetrachord — G A B♭ C 3 minS1

Dorian tetrachord = T-S-T (the half-step sits in the middle). Tap G-A-B♭-C on your lap: "T — short gap — T." This is the Gm7 tetrachord from Ipanema. Say: "Gm7 comes from G Dorian tetrachord." Now alternate Ionian (F) and Dorian (G) taps, eyes closed — feel the shift in where the compression sits. This finger-feel is the Lap Map in action.

Pythagorean Chain — Speak F–C–G–D–A–E–B 4 minS1

Say the chain aloud 3×: "F – C – G – D – A – E – B." Now say it backwards. Identify where F major sits (far left = one flat). Identify C major (no flats, no sharps — adjacent to F). Ask: what one note changes when moving F→C? Answer: B♭ becomes B. Now find G♭ on the chain — it sits opposite F (far right, 6 sharps). This explains the bridge's disorientation: Ipanema doesn't step along the chain, it leaps across it.

5 Key Notes — Analyze Ipanema Verse 4 minS2

Hear before you play. Listen to (or sing) the first 8 bars of the Ipanema verse. Identify: First note (A — the major 3rd of Fmaj7, Zone 1). Highest note (D or E — rises to Zone 2 tension on "watch"). Longest note — find the held note and name its zone. Beat-1 notes — what lands on downbeats? Last note of the phrase (resolution — Zone 1?). Write answers in workbook. This is analysis, not playing yet.

Tiny Tension Arc — Compose in F major 4 minS2

Sing before you play. Over Fmaj7, Zone 1 = F, A, C (chord tones); Zone 2 = G, B♭, D, E (mild tensions). Compose a 4-note motif: Z1 → Z1 → Z2 → Z1. Example: A – C – D – A. Sing it 3× until it's in your ear. Then find it on the guitar (strings 1–3). Play it without looking at your right hand. Add a rhythmic feel: place the Z2 note on an "and" beat for bossa lift.

Son Clave 3-2 — Clap Then Play 4 minS1

Clap first. Bar 1: clap on beat 1, beat 2-and (the "and" of 2), beat 3. Bar 2: clap on beat 1-and, beat 3. Count aloud: "1 — 2 AND 3 — — | — 1 AND — 3 —." Repeat 8 times until it's automatic. Then play open strings on the guitar matching those clap positions. Feel how positions 2, 4, 6, 8 (the "and" beats in the grid) provide the lift. This is the rhythmic DNA of Ipanema.

Shell Voicings — Fmaj7 / Gm7 / C7 on Guitar 4 minS2

Hear before you play. Play Fmaj7 as a shell voicing (root F on string 4, fret 3 + A on string 3 fret 2 + E on string 1 open or fret 0). Sing the top note before each chord. Move to Gm7: root G string 4 fret 5, B♭ string 3 fret 3, F string 1 fret 1. Then C7: C string 5 fret 3, E string 4 fret 2, B♭ string 2 fret 3. Notice: each chord shares at least one note with the next — find the common tone and let it ring. Voice economy: only change what must change.

CAGED First Exposure — E-Form and A-Form for F 2 minS2

F major as E-form barre: barre at fret 1, E-shape behind. F major as A-form: barre fret 1, A-shape (strings 2–4 fret 3). Both are F — same chord, two fingerboard positions. Today: just identify where each sits. Play each once, listen for the difference in register and color. No speed — just map the territory.

Bossa Nova Fingerpicking Pattern — Open Strings 4 minS1

Hear before you play. Bossa nova fingerpicking: thumb (p) plays bass string on beat 1; then fingers play the treble chord on the "and" beats. Basic pattern: p – i+a – m – i+a (thumb on 1, pinch on 1-and, middle on 2-and, pinch on 3-and). On open strings first: p=string 6, i+a=strings 3+1, m=string 2, i+a=strings 3+1. Slow (60 BPM). Count aloud. The thumb alternates strings 6 and 4 once you add chords.

Verse Chords with Fingerpicking — Fmaj7 to Gm7 5 minS2

Play Fmaj7 for 4 bars using the bossa pattern (80 BPM). Thumb alternates string 5 (A) and string 4 (D) for Fmaj7. Then move to G7 for 2 bars — G7 voicing: string 6 fret 3, string 5 fret 2, string 1 fret 1. Then Gm7 to C7 for 2 bars. Keep the fingerpicking pattern consistent through every chord change. Sing before you play — sing the chord root on beat 1 before each change. If the pattern breaks, stop, fix the change, restart.

Add Melody — Ipanema Opening Phrase 5 minS2

Sing before you play. Sing the first phrase ("Tall and tan and young and lovely…") to lock it in your ear. Locate the melody on strings 1–2: A (fret 5, string 1), then the rising phrase. Play melody-only first at 70 BPM. Then combine: bass note on beat 1 (thumb), melody note where it falls, chord fill on "and" beats where melody rests. This is the classic bossa arrangement approach. Do not rush the combination — melody clarity comes first.

Lap Map — Keyboard on Your Hand 3 minS1

F Major Heptachord — LH Eyes Closed 3 minS1

A heptachord = full 7-note scale. LH: thumb on F3, play F-G-A-B♭-C-D-E-F4. Say the Longy syllables aloud: "ta te ta te ta te ta." Now eyes closed: play the heptachord ascending and descending without looking. If a note feels wrong, open eyes — identify which step had the wrong interval, re-play that step, close eyes again. Goal: finger-feel, not visual confirmation.

Pythagorean Chain — Play Adjacent Keys on Piano 4 minS1

Say the chain: "F – C – G – D – A – E – B." Play F major scale, then C major scale (LH). What changed? B♭ → B (one note). Play C major, then G major. What changed? F → F♯. Now play F major, then G♭ major (the bridge key). How many notes changed? Count: 6 of 7 notes are different. Feel that shift in your hand. This is the physical experience of the bridge's disorientation.

Tiny Tension Arc + Chord Tone Improv — F Major Z1 4 minS2

Sing before you play. Zone 1 over Fmaj7 = F, A, C, E (chord tones). Zone 2 = G, B♭, D. Compose a Tiny Tension Arc: Z1→Z1→Z2→Z1. Sing it first (e.g., A–C–D–C). Play it RH only, starting on A4. Son Clave 3-2 — Tap Then Play at Piano 4 minS1

Clap first. Clave Bar 1: beats 1, 2-and, 3. Bar 2: beats 1-and, 3. Tap on the piano fallboard (lid). Then transfer to keyboard: LH plays low F as a single note on each clave hit. Count: "1 — 2 AND 3 — — | — 1 AND — 3 —." Feel how the "AND" beats pull the rhythm forward. Positions 2, 4, 6, 8 in the 8-position grid are where bossa lives. The clave is the skeleton underneath the beautiful voice.

Alberti Bass Intro + Shell Voicings — Piano 4 minS2

Hear before you play. Alberti bass = LH pattern: low note → high note → middle note → high note (e.g., over Fmaj7: F3 – C4 – A3 – C4). Play this under a sustained RH A4. Then try shell voicings as block chords: Fmaj7 (F3–A3–E4), Gm7 (G3–B♭3–F4), C7 (C3–E3–B♭3). Sing the top note of each voicing before playing it. Notice voice economy: the top voice moves F → F → E → B♭ (minimal motion). Small hand movements = beautiful voice leading.

Bridge Preview — Feel G♭ Major Position 2 minS2

Play G♭ major scale (LH): G♭ A♭ B♭ C♭ D♭ E♭ F G♭. Every note except F is a flat or natural (C♭=B). Play Fmaj7, then G♭maj7 (G♭3–B♭3–F♭4 — note F♭=E). Feel the half-step disorientation. Say: "This is what the bridge feels like to the listener." Then return to Fmaj7 — feel the relief. You just experienced the emotional arc of the song's entire harmonic journey in 2 minutes.

Practice: play Fmaj7 for 3.5 beats, then G7 on the half-beat before the bar line. Say: "1-2-3-4-AND | (G7 starts here)." This single rhythmic shift makes the piano suddenly sound like a bossa nova recording. Repeat until it feels natural, not rushed.

Weeks 2–3 — Concept Progression

Week 2 Week 3
Each week deepens what you already know. No new major concepts — only sharpened application. Ipanema threads through every section.
Week 2 Sharpening — Detail and Speed

S1 — Plogger (10 min)

Hear before you play. Lap Map: form any major tetrachord from any white-key starting note, eyes closed. Test: F, G, A, B♭, C, D, E — all Ionian (T-T-S). Then test Dorian from G, A, D. Speed goal: each tetrachord < 10 seconds to form on lap. Pythagorean chain: say it forwards and backwards in under 20 seconds. New drill: "What key has 1 flat? 2 flats? 0?" Answer by locating position on chain.

S2 — Integrated (18 min)

Melody: Play your Tiny Tension Arc at three rhythmic speeds: quarter notes, then dotted-quarter, then half note on the Z2. Notice how Duration Amplifies Zone — the longer you hold Z2, the stronger the tension. Sing before you play every variant.
Rhythm: Add the clave to your playing. Guitar: clap clave with right hand, then strum on clave hits. Piano: LH plays clave hits as low notes while RH holds chord.
Voicings: Fmaj7 → Gm7 → C7 → Fmaj7 as voice-led chords. Top voice must move stepwise or stay. Find the smoothest path.

S3 — Song (14 min)

Guitar: Full verse with bossa fingerpicking at 80 BPM. Add melody notes where possible — even if only 3 per bar. The fingerpicking pattern is the priority. Strive for zero hesitation at chord changes.
Piano: LH anticipations on "and of 4" before each chord change. RH melody clearly audible above the LH texture. Try the full 8-bar verse once at 70 BPM without stopping.
Both: Listen to the original Ipanema recording. Find your melody in it. Identify the bridge entry by ear.

S4–S5 — Jam + Synth (18 min)

Jam: Play the verse loop. Add one new element each minute: (1) plain chords, (2) clave rhythm, (3) one melody note per bar, (4) Zone 2 note on "and" beats, (5) a stop — one beat of silence per phrase. 12-bar blues contrast: play I-I-I-I/IV-IV-I-I/V-IV-I-V in F major (F7–B♭7–F7–F7/B♭7–B♭7–F7–F7/C7–B♭7–F7–C7). Notice: this is about forward motion, not the spine of all music.
Synth: "What did the Lap Map teach my body this week that my eyes couldn't?" Write one sentence.

Week 3 Bridge — New Harmonic Territory

S1 — Plogger (10 min)

Hear before you play. Lap Map: now test black-key starting notes. G♭ Ionian tetrachord on lap (G♭ A♭ B♭ C♭). The gaps feel different — two black keys in a row means the half-step is between adjacent raised positions. This is G♭ territory. Pythagorean chain: add the flat side — B♭ F C G (going left). State: "F has one flat: B♭. B♭ has two flats: B♭ and E♭." This connects the chain to what you play.

S2 — Integrated (18 min)

Sing before you play. 5 Key Notes applied to the bridge: "Ooh, but I watch her so sadly…" Find the First, Highest, Longest, Beat-1, Last notes in the bridge phrase. Which zone are they in, over G♭maj7? Name them.
Tiny Tension Arc in G♭ major: compose Z1→Z1→Z2→Z1 over G♭maj7 (Z1 = G♭, B♭, D♭, F). Sing it. Compare the emotional color to your F major arc — does it feel brighter or more mysterious?
Rhythm: play the clave 2-3 orientation (flip bars 1 and 2). Notice how the weight shifts.

S3 — Song (14 min)

Guitar: Learn the bridge chords (G♭maj7 as barre, B7 as open-position or barre VII, F♯m7, D7). Play bridge chord sequence slowly. Connect verse → bridge → back to verse. Feel the shift when you arrive at G♭ — let it be disorienting. That is the point.
Piano: Play verse (LH shell voicings), then bridge (G♭maj7 position — B♭3–D♭4–F4 shell). The return to Gm7–C7 at bridge end is the resolution. Play the full AABA form: verse (A) twice, bridge (B), verse (A). At 80 BPM, no stopping.

S4–S5 — Jam + Synth (18 min)

Jam: Loop the AABA form. Improvise freely on the bridge using only G♭ Ionian chord tones (Z1). Come home to F major on the final A. Notice: how does your body signal "I'm back in F"? That sensation is tonal center awareness.
Synth: Three questions: (1) How does the Lap Map prepare you for G♭ tetrachords before you touch the instrument? (2) Where in Ipanema does Duration most amplify zone? (3) If you had to explain the bridge to a non-musician, what would you say? Write it out — this builds your musical vocabulary.

Week 4 Jam Prompt — Both Instruments
The Full Journey: AABA at Performance Feel

Hear before you play. Before touching the instrument, close your eyes and hear the entire song — verse, verse, bridge, verse — in your mind at 100 BPM. Hear the bossa groove, the melody arc, the bridge's half-step shift, the return home. Then play it. No metronome. Trust your internal pulse. If you lose the groove, stop — tap the clave — restart from the top of the section.

Minute 1–3: Chords only, groove focus. No melody pressure.
Minute 4–7: Add melody. Keep the groove as the priority.
Minute 8–10: Free — play what feels musical. Stop analyzing.
