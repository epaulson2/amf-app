Musical OS Plogger · Chapter 5 — The Lap Map

Your Hands Are a Keyboard

The Lap Map is the single most powerful silent-practice tool in Plogger. Place your hands palm-down on your lap. Your ten fingertips are the white keys. The spaces between fingers are the black keys. This is a fully functional keyboard you carry everywhere.

Lap Map Finger Layout

Finger (L→R) Left Pinky Left Ring Left Middle Left Index Both Thumbs
White Key C D E F G / A
Space = Black C#/Db D#/Eb — F#/Gb G#/Ab

Note: the gap between E and F (no black key) is felt as fingertips touching. The gap between B and C is felt the same way on the right hand.

The Four Tetrachord Feels

A tetrachord is a group of four notes spanning a perfect fourth. Each type has a distinct fingertip-spacing pattern — a physical "feel" you can memorize without an instrument.

Major Tetrachord

W–W–H (whole, whole, half). Example: C–D–E–F.
Feel: two wide gaps, then a close squeeze.

S1 · Discovering — Hear Before You Play
Hum C–D–E–F on the piano (or any melody source) until you feel the brightness. Then map it on your lap. Eyes closed — three fingers wide apart, last finger tucked close.

Minor Tetrachord

W–H–W (whole, half, whole). Example: A–B–C–D.
Feel: wide gap, close squeeze, then wide again.

S1 · Discovering — Hear Before You Play
Hum A–B–C–D on the piano. Notice the darkness compared to major. Find the "close squeeze" in the middle on your lap fingers.

Phrygian Tetrachord

H–W–W (half, whole, whole). Example: E–F–G–A.
Feel: immediate tight squeeze at the start, then two wide steps.

S2 · Perfecting — Hear Before You Play
Hum E–F–G–A. The half-step opening gives it a Spanish/flamenco flavor. Map it: first two fingers close, then two wide steps outward.

Lydian Tetrachord

W–W–W (all whole steps). Example: C–D–E–F#.
Feel: three consistently wide gaps — even spacing throughout.

S2 · Perfecting — Hear Before You Play
Hum C–D–E–F#. The augmented fourth (tritone) at the top gives this its dreamy, floating quality. Three equal gaps on your lap.
S3 · Fluent — Sprint 3 Lap Map Target
Exit Criterion: From any starting note named by a partner, you can form a major or minor tetrachord on your lap within 3 seconds, eyes away from your hands. Practice this on the bus, at a table, anywhere. The instrument is optional — the Lap Map is always with you.
Why tetrachords matter for Ipanema: The F major scale is built from two major tetrachords stacked: F–G–A–Bb (lower) + C–D–E–F (upper). When Jobim shifts to Gb major in the bridge, he's stacking two Gb major tetrachords: Gb–Ab–Bb–Cb + Db–Eb–F–Gb. Feeling each tetrachord on your lap before the shift helps you hear the key change before the chord sounds.

The Spine of All Keys: F–C–G–D–A–E–B

Seven letters. One sequence. This is the order in which notes were discovered by tuning in perfect fifths — each note a fifth above the previous. Memorize it left-to-right.

The Fundamental Rule: Adjacent Notes Share 6 of 7

Move one step right on the spine (e.g., F major → C major): you add one sharp, remove one flat, and keep the other six notes identical. Move one step left: add one flat, remove one sharp, keep six.

F major ← F G A Bb C D E Our home key for the verse

G major G A B C D E F# F → F# (one note changes)

The Heptachord Shift — Why the Bridge Feels So Far Away

The bridge of Ipanema begins on Gb major. F major and Gb major are not adjacent on the Pythagorean spine — they are on opposite ends, separated by the entire chromatic space. This means they share only one note (approximately). That is why the bridge sounds like you've been teleported.

Heptachord Shift — The Bridge in Plain Language
F major (verse)
F · G · A · Bb · C · D · E

The E natural (scale degree 7) is your anchor — you feel completely at home.

Gb major (bridge)
Gb · Ab · Bb · Cb · Db · Eb · F

Almost every note has changed. Only the Bb and F survive. Everything else floats free.

The Magic: Jobim lands on Gbmaj7 — one half-step above F — and holds it for four full bars. Your ear loses its footing completely. When Gm7–C7 arrives again, the return to F feels like waking from a dream. This is the Heptachord Shift: a move so far out that only the full return can resolve it.

Melody Chamber 5 Key Notes · Tiny Tension Arc · Duration Amplifies Zone

The 5 Key Notes Framework

Every melody can be understood through five structural notes. These notes reveal the intent of the phrase before you analyze a single scale degree.

Key Note What It Tells You In Ipanema (Verse Opening)
1. First Note Establishes the zone (Z1–Z4). Opens a question or a statement. A (scale degree 3 of F) → Zone 1 Sweet. Opens from home.
2. Last Note Resolved = closed phrase. Open (tension) = phrase continues. Verse phrases often end on F (1) or C (5) — closed resolution.
3. Highest Note Melodic peak = emotional peak. Where is it relative to chord? F (scale degree 1 on Fmaj7) — peak feels like arriving, not straining.
4. Longest Note Duration amplifies the tension of the zone. Long Z3 = big tension. Long Z1 = deep rest. The "tall and tan" phrase holds its note — duration = settling weight.
5. Beat-1 Note Strongest rhythmic position. Z1 on Beat 1 = grounded. Z3 on Beat 1 = urgent. Ipanema verse lands on Beat 1 with chord tones — rhythmic security.

Example in F major: Play A (3) → C (5) → D (6) → C (5).
A and C are Zone 1 (chord tones). D is Zone 4 (the "LA note" — 6th degree, context-dependent but mildly dreamy over Fmaj7). C resolves.
This is almost exactly how Jobim opens the Ipanema verse.
S1 · Discovering — Hear Before You Play
Exercise 1: On your instrument, find A–C–D–C in F major. Play it slowly. Hear the arrival at A (home), the confirmation at C, the slight float at D, and the comfortable return to C. That float on D is all the tension this melody needs to feel alive.
S2 · Perfecting — Hear Before You Play
Exercise 2: Compose your own Z1–Z1–Z2–Z1 arc in G major (G, B, D are Z1; A, E are Z2). Play it over a Gmaj7 chord. Then move it to Dm7 and notice how the same arc changes emotional color — same pattern, different chord = different feeling.

Duration Amplifies Zone

The longer a note lasts, the more its zone quality is amplified. A quick Z3 note creates a passing sting. A held Z3 note creates anguish. A sustained Z1 note creates deep peace.

Pattern: I–I–I–I / IV–IV–I–I / V–IV–I–V. Every chord is a dominant 7th (adds bluesy b7). This is the most predictable 12-bar pattern in music — knowing it lets you instantly hear how Jobim departs from convention.

Ipanema Harmony — Verse Analysis

The verse lives in F major. But it doesn't use a textbook I–IV–V progression. Jobim creates an 8-bar world with only three distinct harmonic areas.

Fmaj7
F · A · C · E
Bars 1–4 (I)
Pure tonic. 4 full bars of home.
G7
G · B · D · F
Bars 5–6 (borrowed)

Gm7
G · Bb · D · F

Minor ii chord — soft approach.

Dominant pull back to F.

What the G7 Actually Does

G7 is not the V7 of F (that would be C7). It is the V7 of IV — a secondary dominant that briefly points toward Bb major (IV of F), then Jobim redirects with Gm7–C7 back home. This creates a subtle sense of harmonic wandering: you leave home, look toward a neighboring key, and return. Four bars of Fmaj7 make this departure feel dramatic despite lasting only two bars.

Voice Economy Through Fmaj7 Changes

With four bars on Fmaj7, you have time to explore inversions rather than re-striking the same voicing. Voice economy means: when changing chord, move the minimum number of voices the minimum distance.

Fmaj7 → Gm7: Minimal Voice Movement

Voice Fmaj7 (bar 7 prep) Gm7 (bar 7) Movement
SopranoE (maj7)F (b7 of Gm7)+1 half step
AltoC (5)D (5 of Gm7)+1 whole step
TenorA (3)Bb (b3 of Gm7)+1 half step
BassF (root)G (root)+1 whole step

Every voice moves by a step or half-step. No leaps. This is voice economy in action.

6 Moments to Listen For

1. The 4-Bar Fmaj7 Float

Four bars of pure tonic — almost nowhere else in the standard repertoire stays home this long. Jobim uses it to let the melody breathe without harmonic movement underneath. Notice how the melody still creates tension through Duration and Zone alone.

2. The G7 Surprise

After four bars of pure F, the G7 sounds slightly "wrong" — brighter than expected, a bit outside. This is intentional. Jobim borrowed V7/IV to create the harmonic equivalent of a cloud crossing the sun. It lasts two bars, just long enough to unsettle you.

3. The "Ahh" on C7

The phrase "each one she passes goes — ahh" lands on a Z3 (4th scale degree) over C7. Duration amplifies this tension. The held "ahh" is the most tense moment in the verse. Listen for it to feel like it needs to fall somewhere.

4. The Bridge Landing

When Gbmaj7 arrives, your ear has no anchor. Every chord tone you expect has shifted a half-step. Listen for the moment you stop feeling "home." This is the Heptachord Shift landing.

5. The Bossa Nova "Lift"

In the bridge, notice the rhythm pushes forward — chords anticipate the beat by an eighth note. This is the "and" before beat 1 landing early. That slight rhythmic lean is what makes bossa nova feel like walking in slow forward lean rather than marching.

6. The Return to Gm7–C7

After B7 and F#m7 and D7 — three chords that sound increasingly "lost" — the Gm7–C7 arrives like firm ground under your feet. This is why Jobim spends so long in Gb. The longer you drift, the sweeter the return.

S1 · Discovering — Hear Before You Play
Clave Exercise: Play a recording of Ipanema. Clap on positions 1, 4, 6 of bar 1 and positions 3, 7 of bar 2. You will feel the clave either lining up or creating cross-rhythm against the music. The clave is not always stated explicitly — sometimes it is implied by the groove.

Rhythm Code Positions 2, 4, 6, 8 — The Lift

In the 8-position grid, odd positions (1,3,5,7) are the beats. Even positions (2,4,6,8) are the "ands" — the off-beats. In bossa nova and all syncopated music, notes on even positions push the rhythm forward. They create the feeling of leaning into the next beat.

S1 · Discovering — Guitar Fingerpicking Pattern: Hear Before You Play

Hear this pattern on a bossa nova recording before attempting it — the right hand must feel loose and circular, never tense.

Voicings Chamber Deepening Inversions · Fmaj7 Voice Economy

No New Voicings This Sprint — Going Deeper

Sprint 3 introduces no new voicing types. Instead, you will deepen inversions using the Fmaj7 changes as material. The principle: when you already know the notes of a chord, true mastery means moving through all four inversions fluidly, choosing the one that best connects to the next chord by voice economy.

Four Inversions of Fmaj7 — Side by Side

Inversion Bass Note Notes (low → high) Use When
Root Position F F · A · C · E Opening statement. Establishing the tonic firmly.
1st Inversion A A · C · E · F Floating, less settled. Good for inner bars where motion is implied.
2nd Inversion C C · E · F · A Slightly ambiguous, dreamy. Connects smoothly to Gm7 (C is common tone).
3rd Inversion E E · F · A · C Very unstable in bass. Creates forward motion — E wants to move up to F or down to D.
S2 · Perfecting — Hear Before You Play
Inversion Exercise: Play Fmaj7 in root position. Hear it settle. Then play 1st inversion — notice it feels less anchored. 2nd inversion — dreamy. 3rd inversion — feels unstable, like it needs to go somewhere. Now play: Root → 3rd inversion → Gm7 root position. The 3rd inversion creates a natural "lean" into the Gm7. This is voice economy in practice.
Voice Economy Rule: Choose the inversion that minimizes total voice travel. Fmaj7 2nd inv (C–E–F–A) → Gm7 root (G–Bb–D–F): each voice moves ≤2 half-steps — average 1.5. That is efficient voice leading.

Guitar: CAGED System — First Exposure

CAGED names the five barre chord shapes (C, A, G, E, D). This sprint you only need to know Fmaj7's two primary positions:

Fmaj7 — "E-shape" barre (1st fret)

Index barre across fret 1. Middle finger fret 2, string 3. Ring finger fret 3, string 5. Pinky fret 3, string 4. The "E shape" moved up one fret. This is the primary bossa nova Fmaj7 voicing.

S1 · Discovering — Hear Before You Play
Before fingering the chord, hum F–A–C–E on piano. Then form the shape with your fretting hand. Play each string separately to check clarity before strumming.

Fmaj7 — "A-shape" barre (8th fret)

Barre fret 8. Ring finger barre fret 10 (strings 2–4). Leave string 1 open (E = maj7 of F). Higher register, brighter sound. This is CAGED's A-shape for F.

S2 · Perfecting — Hear Before You Play
Play the E-shape version, then the A-shape version. Hear how the same chord voicing sounds darker lower, brighter higher. This is what CAGED gives you: the same chord family across the entire fretboard.

Piano: Alberti Bass Introduction
