Week 1 — Learning for the Day

Read this entire page before your first practice session of the week. These are the ideas your exercises will build.

Musical OS — Plogger

Ch.3: Every Note Has a Landmark

The keyboard is not a random row of keys. Black keys come in groups of 2 and 3. Every white note lives next to a specific black-key group. D is the lone white key between the two black keys. F is the first white key left of the three black keys. Once your eyes (and fingers) lock onto these landmarks, you can find any note in under 2 seconds — and eventually with your eyes closed.

Week 1 goal: Name and locate C, D, E, F, G, A, B instantly. Use the black-key groups as anchors, not note names alone.

Musical OS — Plogger Ch.4

Longy Rhythms: The Speaking System

Every rhythm has a spoken syllable. ta = quarter note. ta-te = two eighth notes. ta-fe-te-fe = four sixteenth notes. ta---te = dotted quarter + eighth. This week: master ta, ta-te, and ta-fe-te-fe. Speak every rhythm before you clap it, and clap before you play it.

Hear before you play — even rhythm lives in your inner ear first.

Melody Chamber — Stability Ladder

Four Zones Over Any Chord

Every note you could play over a chord sits in one of four zones: Zone 1 (Sweet) — chord tones, always stable. Zone 2 (Tension) — half-step above a chord tone, maximum pull. Zone 3 (Color) — whole step above, adds flavor without urgency. Zone 4 (Passing) — moves through quickly, doesn't land. Week 1: identify zones on the Am7 and Gmaj7 chords in Autumn Leaves.

Melody Chamber — Chord-Change Behaviors

What Happens to Your Note When the Chord Changes?

Stay Sweet
Your note was a chord tone. New chord arrives. Your note is still a chord tone. Nothing changed — pure smoothness.
Turn Tense
Your note was sweet. New chord arrives. Your note is now a half-step clash. The music wants you to move.
Float Free
Your note was sweet. New chord arrives. Your note is now a whole-step color tone. You have freedom — stay or move.
Sink Deeper
Your note was already color. New chord arrives. Your note becomes even more unstable. Strong pull to resolve.

This week: hear each behavior on Am7→D7. Name it out loud. Sing before you play.

Harmony Chamber — New This Week

I-IV-V-I: The Fundamental Cadence

In G major: G – C – D – G. This is the foundation of Western harmony. The I chord is home. IV is a step away — warmer, fuller. V is tension — it aches to return. I resolves. This is not a drill vehicle — it is an emotional arc. Every time you play it, feel the journey: home → away → longing → home.

Also introduced: I-V-vi-IV in G major: G – D – Em – C. The "four chord song." Notice the vi (Em) — that is the relative minor, the same world as E minor in Autumn Leaves. Same notes, different gravity.

Autumn Leaves — Week 1 Thread

The First Four Chords: Am7 → D7 → Gmaj7 → Cmaj7

This is the ii-V-I-IV in G major. Listen to a recording and follow these four chords. The Am7 is the ii (two chord) — it sets up tension. D7 is the V7 — maximum pull. Gmaj7 is home in G major. Cmaj7 is the warm IV. Every drill this week connects back to this four-chord passage.

Hear before you play — listen to the first 8 bars of Autumn Leaves before every practice session this week.

Zone Identification on Guitar Neck Stage 1 5 min

Sing before you play. Sing "Am7" then hum each chord tone (A-C-E-G) before touching the guitar.

  • Hold Am7 chord. Identify: root (A), third (C), fifth (E), seventh (G) — these are Zone 1 (Sweet)
  • On high E string: walk up from open E — name each note's zone over Am7 (open E = Zone 1 fifth; F = Zone 2; G = Zone 1 seventh; A = Zone 1 root)
  • Repeat same walk over Gmaj7 chord — same string, new zones
  • Name each zone out loud as you play it: "sweet, tense, sweet, color..."
Chord-Change Behavior — Am7 to D7 Stage 2 5 min

Hear before you play. Sing the note G (the 7th of Am7). Hold it in your head. Now mentally land on D7 — what happens?

  • Play G note on high E (3rd fret). Strum Am7 — G is Zone 1 (Sweet). Strum D7 — G is now the minor 7th... Float Free (whole step above F#).
  • Now try E (5th fret high E). Am7: Zone 1. D7: Zone 2 (half step above chord tone D — Turn Tense!)
  • Name the behavior aloud each time: "Stay Sweet / Turn Tense / Float Free / Sink Deeper"
  • Connect to Autumn Leaves: the melody note in bar 1 — which behavior does it experience at the chord change?
I-IV-V-I in G Major — Guitar Shapes Stage 1 5 min

Hear before you play. Hum G-C-D-G in your head. Feel the pull of D wanting to go back to G.

G (open) → C (x32010) → D (xx0232) → G (open)
  • Play each chord as a simple strum, 4 beats each, at 70 BPM
  • Strum down only — feel the arc: home → fuller → tension → home
  • Say the Roman numeral as you play: "I... IV... V... I..."
  • Then: I-V-vi-IV = G → D → Em → C — play same way, say numerals aloud
Voicings Deepening — Am7 Inversions Stage 2 3 min

Sing before you play. Sing A-C-E-G (Am7 tones) before playing any inversion.

  • Root position Am7: x02010. Identify root (A) in bass.
  • First inversion (C in bass): x32010-like shape — find it on neck, confirm C is lowest sounding note
  • No jumping more than a 4th between chord changes. Voice-lead: keep common tones where possible

Keyboard Landmark Eyes-Closed Drill Stage 1 4 min

Hear before you play. Before touching keys, visualize the full keyboard in your mind — see the black-key groups.

  • Eyes open: play middle C, D, E, F, G, A, B using only black-key group anchors (no reading names)
  • Eyes closed: place one finger on D (the white key between the two black keys). Confirm. Now find F. G. A.
  • Week 1 goal: all 7 white keys in random order, eyes closed, under 2 seconds each
  • Use Plogger di-chord naming: "D is the lone key, C is its left neighbor" — say it aloud
Longy Speak + Keyboard Stage 1 3 min

Hear before you play. Hum the Autumn Leaves melody, then convert it to Longy syllables before touching the keys.

  • Speak "ta ta-te ta ta" while tapping your knee — get the syllables precise
  • Now play a single note (G, middle octave) with RH and speak each syllable as it lands
  • Then apply the rhythm to scale walk: C-D-E-F with ta-ta-ta-te pattern
Heptachord Formation — Am and G Major Stage 1 3 min
  • RH: play A-B-C-D-E-F-G (A natural minor heptachord) slowly, speaking each note aloud
  • LH: same exercise, one octave lower — left hand shapes mirror the right
  • Then G major: G-A-B-C-D-E-F#, both hands, say each note name using landmark system
  • This is not a scale drill — it is a landscape survey. You are mapping the territory of Autumn Leaves.

Zone Mapping on Piano — Am7 and Gmaj7 Stage 1 5 min

Sing before you play. Hum the tones A-C-E-G (Am7). Sing them before touching keys.

  • LH: hold Am7 block chord (A-C-E-G). RH: walk up white keys starting from A, naming each zone
  • A = Zone 1 (root / Sweet). B = Zone 3 (Color). C = Zone 1 (third). D = Zone 3. E = Zone 1 (fifth). F = Zone 2 (Tense). G = Zone 1 (seventh)
  • Speak each zone as you land: "Sweet... Color... Sweet... Color... Sweet... Tense... Sweet..."
  • Repeat over Gmaj7: what changes? What stays the same?
Chord-Change Behavior — Piano Slow Motion Stage 2 5 min

Hear before you play. Hold the melody note in your inner ear before the chord changes under it.

  • Hold G (right hand, single note) while LH plays Am7 → D7 slowly
  • G over Am7: Zone 1 (seventh — Sweet). G over D7: Zone 4 (minor 7th — Float Free)
  • Now try E: Am7 = Zone 1 (fifth). D7 = Zone 2 (half-step above D# enharmonic — Turn Tense)
  • Name behavior aloud at each change: "Float Free... Turn Tense..."
  • Apply to Autumn Leaves bar 1: what behavior does the actual melody note experience?
I-IV-V-I in G Major — Shell Voicings LH Stage 1 5 min

Hear before you play. Sing G-C-D-G before playing. Feel home → away → tension → home.

  • LH only: G (root, low) → C (root) → D (root) → G. Simple root bass, 4 beats each
  • Add shell: LH plays root + fifth for each chord (G-D, C-G, D-A, G-D)
  • RH: add the third on top (G chord = B on top; C chord = E; D chord = F#; G = B)
  • Say Roman numerals as you play: "I... IV... V... I..."
  • Then I-V-vi-IV: G → D → Em → C — same process. Name vi as "the relative minor world of Autumn Leaves"
Shell Voicings — Am7 Sprint 1 Deepening Stage 2 3 min

Sing before you play. Sing A-C-G (Am7 shell) before playing it.

  • Am7 shell: A (LH) — C-G (RH, 3rd and 7th). This is the "open" voicing — no fifth, maximum clarity
  • Voice-lead to D7 shell: D (LH) — C-F# (RH). Notice C stays → float. F# rises to resolve G
  • To Gmaj7 shell: G (LH) — F#-B (RH). One step: C resolves to B. Economy of motion.
  • Practice Am7 → D7 → Gmaj7 as a three-chord loop, RH never jumping more than a 4th

Plogger Ch.4 — Week 2 Push

Dotted Rhythms: ta---te

Dotted quarter = 1.5 beats. Speak "ta---te" with a long hold on the "ta". The dash = time passing. The "te" = the short eighth that snaps the phrase closed. Apply to Autumn Leaves bar 5 (the dotted quarter pick-up). Sing before you play — hear the long-short before speaking it.

Melody — Week 2

All Four Behaviors in E Minor Cycle

Week 1 explored behaviors on Am7→D7. Week 2: apply to F#m7♭5 → B7 → Em. This is the darker second half of Autumn Leaves.

  • The B7 has an active tritone (D#-A) — notes near there will Turn Tense dramatically
  • Em resolution: what zone does your note land in after B7 resolves?
  • Hear before you play — sing the E minor arrival before playing it
