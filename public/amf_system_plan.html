Section 1

The Three-Book System

The AMF is organized as three connected books, each doing one specific job. The Textbook explains; the Practice Manual directs; the Workbook records. Together they form a complete learning architecture.

Book 1

The Adaptable Musician's Framework

The "Why and What" book

Deep theory, examples from world music history, cross-system analysis, the Convergence Matrix. Work through at your own pace — a chapter at a time. Return to it as a reference. Does not tell you what to do today; tells you how music works.

Book 2

AMF Daily Practice Manual

The "What to do today" book

12 monthly sprints, 1-hour sessions per instrument (Piano and Guitar). Every session sequenced so 15, 30, or 60 minutes all work. Audiation-first throughout. Spaced repetition built in. Song learning as a non-negotiable thread. Milestones at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months.

Book 3

AMF Workbook

The "Where I do the work" book

Analysis templates, transcription grids, composition pages, progress tracking, definition-of-done checklists. Companion to both other books. Primarily blank pages designed for writing in.

Section 2

The Four Dimensions of Music

Every musical moment can be described through four independent dimensions. The textbook teaches each system in depth, then reveals what happens when they interact.

Melody OS

The Emotional Map of Melody

Notes are not just pitches — they are functional labels (DO–TI) that carry emotional weight determined by their position in the key AND their relationship to the current chord.

Source: "The Emotional Map of Melody" by Tamas Bodzsar (EMM p.1–3)
Harmony OS

The Harmony Universe

Chords function in groups (Tonic / Subdominant / Dominant), create movement through ii–V–I cycles, and can borrow from parallel modes to expand emotional range.

Source: theharmonyproject.io
Voicings OS

The Texture Layer

The same chord voiced differently creates a completely different emotional experience. Guide tones, position theory (P1/P2/P3), and available tensions are the tools.

Source: theharmonyproject.io
Rhythm Code

The Time Dimension

Rhythm = starting points, not duration. The son clave — a 5-note, 2-measure pattern found in every genre — underlies most great grooves. The Rhythm Code Map shows all 16 positions rated Strong, Medium, or Avoid.

Source: "The Rhythm Code" by Tamas Bodzsar (RC p.9–121)

Section 4

Book 2 — AMF Daily Practice Manual

One Hour Per Instrument. Sequenced by Priority.

Each session is structured so that stopping at 15, 30, or 60 minutes always means you did the most important work first. Piano and Guitar are separate 1-hour plans — you can do one per day, both, or alternate by day.

Priority Time Block If You Have… What You Do
Priority 1 Minutes 0–15 15 min or more Ear Training (audiation drill)  ·  Quick song phrase  ·  Spaced review of one past concept
Priority 2 Minutes 15–30 30 min or more Technical drill for today's concept (instrument-specific)  ·  Voicing or chord shape work
Priority 3 Minutes 30–45 45 min or more Full current repertoire song  ·  Work problem sections  ·  Sight-reading one new phrase
Priority 4 Minutes 45–60 Full hour Free improvisation/exploration with today's concept  ·  Documentation (what you discovered)
Piano Session vs. Guitar Session — Key Differences

Piano

  • Voicings in both hands — chord spread, register, pedaling
  • Left hand rhythm follows Rhythm Code clave
  • Tensions voiced explicitly across keys
  • Improvisation: zone-based right-hand melody over left-hand vamp
  • Ear training focus: hear voicing quality before playing

Guitar

  • Chord shapes, capo use, barre vs. open positions
  • Strumming/fingerpicking pattern carries the groove
  • Tensions implied through partial shapes and melody notes
  • Lead playing: zone navigation on the fretboard
  • Ear training focus: hear the melody and find it on the neck
Section 5

Three Definitions of Done

Not all skills are learned the same way. Some things require strict mastery before moving forward. Others are lifelong practices where the goal is measurable growth, not perfection. And repertoire has its own standard — performance readiness.

Definition of Done Type 1

Mastery (Strict)

✓ Must achieve before progressing

These are foundational, discrete skills where precision matters. You either can do it or you can't. Examples: identifying all 7 scale degrees by ear in real time, playing a ii–V–I with correct voicings in 3 keys, clapping the 2-3 and 3-2 son clave accurately.

Standard: "Can perform this correctly and consistently, 9 out of 10 attempts, without hesitation, in multiple keys."

What "done" looks like: A specific, testable action you perform and pass.

Definition of Done Type 2

Progressive (Growth)

↑ Measured by growth over time

These are complex, integrated skills that are never "finished" — they deepen over a lifetime. Examples: inner ear → instrument transfer speed, improvisation quality, zone awareness while playing, creative use of divergence.

Standard: "At minimum, you should be able to [specific observable behavior]. Full fluency develops over months and years."

What "done" looks like: A minimum bar for each time period (1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year), with honest acknowledgment that the skill continues to deepen.

Definition of Done Type 3

Performance Ready

♪ Ready for a real audience

For repertoire songs. The standard is not theoretical knowledge of the song — it's the ability to perform it live.

Standard: "Can play the complete song from memory, at a comfortable tempo, in front of another person, without stopping to correct mistakes. Transitions are smooth. The song communicates emotionally."

What "done" looks like: You've played it for someone. Even once. Even informally.

Section 7

Milestone Checkpoints

These are not tests. They are honest calibration points — a structured self-assessment to confirm where you are and what to focus on next.

Milestone 1

End of Month 1

Mastery Checks

  • Can sing the Stability Ladder (DO–SO–MI–LA–RE–TI–FA) from memory, in any key
  • Can label any of the 7 scale degrees by its zone without hesitation
  • Can clap both the 2-3 and 3-2 son clave accurately over a recording
  • Can identify chord function (Tonic / Subdominant / Dominant) in a simple 4-chord loop

Progressive Minimums

  • Can play "Ain't No Sunshine" beginning to end without stopping (even slowly)
  • Has noticed at least one moment of zone-rhythm interaction while listening
  • Has applied the AMF Perception Protocol (all 5 layers) to at least 2 songs

Repertoire

  • "Ain't No Sunshine" — Performance Ready
  • "What's Going On" — can play/sing through, not yet performance ready

Milestone 2

End of Month 3

Mastery Checks

  • Can identify the clave orientation (2-3 or 3-2) in any anchor song by ear
  • Can play a ii–V–I with correct 7th chord voicings in 3 keys on piano
  • Can play the same ii–V–I cleanly on guitar in 2 keys
  • Can identify secondary dominants and tritone substitutions by ear

Progressive Minimums

  • Inner ear → instrument: hear a zone and find it within 5 seconds
  • Can describe any important note as a 2D event (Zone / Function)
  • Has completed Textbook Chapters 1–5

Repertoire (5 songs performance-ready)

  • Ain't No Sunshine  ·  Superstition  ·  Hallelujah
  • Autumn Leaves  ·  What's Going On

Milestone 3

End of Month 6

Mastery Checks

  • Can describe any musical moment as a complete 4D Event (Zone / Function / Voicing / Rhythm position)
  • Can complete the full 5-layer AMF Perception Protocol within 3 minutes
  • Can play guide-tone voicings (3rd + 7th) for any chord type in any key on piano

Progressive Minimums

  • Inner ear → instrument: hear a phrase in your head and find it without notation
  • Can improvise a 4-bar melody that uses at least 2 different zones consciously
  • Has completed Textbook Chapters 1–10

Repertoire (10 songs performance-ready)

  • All Month 1–3 songs, plus: Just the Two of Us  ·  Yesterday
  • Girl from Ipanema  ·  My Funny Valentine  ·  Blackbird  ·  Watermelon Man

Milestone 4

End of Month 12

Mastery Checks

  • Can improvise meaningfully over any repertoire song using zone awareness consciously
  • Can compose an 8-bar section using the 7-step 4D Composition Process and explain every decision
  • Can play all 20 repertoire songs on at least one instrument

Progressive Minimums

  • Inner ear → instrument: hear a chord voicing with tension color and play it within seconds
  • Can identify harmonic-rhythmic framework in a live situation within 60 seconds
  • Has completed all 18 Textbook chapters + at least 5 of 10 Workshops

Repertoire (20 songs)

  • All 20 AMF Starter Repertoire songs — minimum 15 performance-ready
  • At least 2 "Oddballs" from the Expand list
Section 8

The Core Skill — Inner Ear to Instrument

One of the most consistently underdeveloped skills in musicians who learn theoretically is audiation transfer — the ability to hear a musical idea in your head and immediately play it on the instrument. The AMF Practice Manual addresses this directly. It is the one thread that runs through every single session, every sprint, every month.

Stage 1

Zone Audiation

Month 1–2

Hear a scale degree label (e.g., "TI") → Sing it in your head → Find it on the instrument

Progressive DoD: Within 5 seconds, in any key
Stage 2

Rhythm Audiation

Month 2–3

Hear a rhythmic pattern (clave, stop, anticipation) → Clap it → Play it

Mastery DoD: Can clap and play both clave orientations at any tempo
Stage 3

Chord Quality Audiation

Month 4–5

Hear a chord quality (Imaj7, V7, ii-7) → Sing the sound → Play the voicing

Progressive DoD: Can place a voicing within 10 seconds of hearing the chord label
Stage 4

Full Phrase Transfer

Month 6+

Hear a complete musical phrase in your head (zone + rhythm + harmony) → Play it

Progressive DoD: Can translate an imagined phrase without notation, within a musically useful timeframe
